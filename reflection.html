Perplexity Summary — Pain Point Research

The following is a structured summary of the most common headphone pain points identified across major premium models including AirPods, Beats, Sony WH-1000XM5, and Bose QuietComfort, drawn from an analysis of 14,000+ customer reviews.


Top Pain Points Identified:


1. Comfort & Pressure (92%) — Headphones feel heavy, clamp too tightly, or cause ear/head pressure during long sessions. "The headphones are very good but uncomfortable after an hour."

2. Bluetooth Connectivity Drops (88%) — Audio cuts out, devices disconnect, or pairing is unreliable. "After initial Bluetooth connection I experienced no less than 4 connectivity issues."

3. Battery Life & Charging (81%) — Battery drains faster than expected, case charging is problematic, or longevity declines over time. "AirPods Case battery drain scared me."

4. ANC Not Meeting Expectations (79%) — Active Noise Cancellation doesn't block enough noise in real-world settings or performs inconsistently. "The noise cancellation is worse than the Bose QuietComfort Ultra 2."

5. Price vs. Value (74%) — Customers feel the sound, comfort, or feature set doesn't justify the premium price. "The price point didn't align with their expectations."

6. Sound Quality Tuning (70%) — Bass is too strong, vocals lack clarity, or sound doesn't match the price. "Too bassy and slightly overwhelming."

7. Controls & Usability (62%) — Touch controls misfire, buttons are awkward, or on-device controls feel unreliable. "When I tried to fast-forward, it instead skipped to the next track."

8. App & Software Issues (58%) — Companion apps look outdated, are buggy, or add friction. "The Sony app looks ugly."

9. Durability & Build Quality (55%) — Hinges, earpads, or long-term wear feel fragile. "Durability was a common pain point across premium models."

10. Fit & Ear Compatibility (51%) — Earbuds don't sit right, rotate, or feel uncomfortable for certain ear shapes. "They are bulky, and the shape seems to favor some ears over others."


Beyond product features, the research was expanded to address real hearing health risks including noise-induced hearing loss, tinnitus, age-related hearing loss, background noise masking, listening fatigue, and speech clarity gaps — identifying key customer segments: Casual Listeners, Daily Commuters, users with Mild Hearing Loss, Tinnitus/Sensitive users, Older Listeners (50+), and Heavy/Pro users.

Question 01
In completing the task, what steps worked well?
What worked best for me was approaching the research from two perspectives simultaneously — not just from the company's internal point of view, but also as an outside consumer. This dual lens helped me ask better questions about how we could actually obtain this data and what key segments we needed to focus on.

From top to bottom, we redefined the way we built the pain point chart. Rather than accepting surface-level complaints, we pushed deeper — taking the volume of reviews we gathered and converting that raw data into meaningful percentages tied to specific, recurring issues. This turned anecdotal feedback into structured, visual evidence.

The most effective step was expanding the research beyond a standard pain point list to identify who we are actually working with — breaking customers into real segments and understanding what each group specifically needs. That shift from general complaints to targeted insights made the entire chart far more valuable and actionable.
Question 02
What roadblocks did you encounter along the way?
The main roadblock was a strategic decision point early in the process: do we stop at building a basic pain point chart, or do we push ourselves to go further — gathering more data, finding deeper insights, and producing something more meaningful and complete?

A second challenge was tool limitation. Perplexity AI was used initially to pull customer review data, but it struggled to fully understand the scope and direction of what we were trying to achieve. It provided general summaries but lacked the depth and context needed to really drive the research forward in a focused way.
Question 03
How did you solve those issues?
We solved the strategic challenge by choosing to push further — committing to deeper research rather than stopping at the minimum. This decision is what elevated the output from a simple chart to a full customer insights report.

For the tool limitation, we transitioned to Claude — a more capable LLM — specifically because it demonstrated a stronger understanding of the research goal. Where Perplexity summarized, Claude analyzed. It was able to synthesize the data into structured findings, identify patterns across customer segments, build frequency percentages from review volume, and surface the hearing health dimension of the research that we felt was the real opportunity for Beats.

The decision to use Claude was not made lightly — it was a deliberate choice based on the tool's ability to go deeper, think in context, and help us produce research that matched the ambition of the project rather than limiting it.
